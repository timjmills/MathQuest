# Lane D r12 research notes: area models with blank edges, and the forms of a division problem

Sources consulted from the builder's working knowledge of the sites' public worksheet indexes (MathWorksheets4Kids
"area model multiplication" / "area model division", K5 Learning "area model multiplication", IXL "multiply using
area models" / "divide using area models"). Nothing copied; grammar only.

1. MW4K box-method multiplication runs in two tiers: edges printed (the pupil fills the partial products and the total),
   then edges blank ("decompose and fill"): the pupil writes the expanded parts on the edges first. Same grid both tiers.
2. K5 Learning's area-model pages give the expanded parts on the edges throughout; the blank-edge form appears as the
   "fill in the missing numbers" variant. So blank edges are the later, harder step - our "Pupil writes them".
3. IXL multiplies with an area model by asking for the edge parts as typed boxes first, then the partial areas, then
   the sum; every box graded. Our screen does the same: edge, part and total inputs all graded.
4. Division area models (MW4K, IXL "divide using area models"): the divisor sits on the side, the areas (dividend split)
   inside, the quotient's parts are the missing top lengths; a harder form leaves the side length blank as well.
5. The original problem is printed in three ways across these sites: 84 ÷ 4, the long-division bracket, and 84 over 4
   (fraction bar). Mixed-notation pages are a recognised "read every form of division" exercise.
6. Our differences (the standards win): black and white, one model per boxed cell, boxes >= 6 mm, at most 6 a page;
   mixed edges are dealt from the seeded page deck so a page of 4+ is never all one kind.
7. Misconceptions to build from: writing the multiplicand's digits on the edges (3 and 6, not 30 and 6); a dropped zero
   in a partial product; adding the edge parts instead of the areas; reading the fraction form as dividing 4 by 84.
