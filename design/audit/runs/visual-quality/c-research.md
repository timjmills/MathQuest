# Visual-quality lane, explorer (c): how strong K-6 materials draw representations and answer areas

Research only. I took the content model from these sites and copied no wording or artwork. Our contract (WORKSHEET_DESIGN_STANDARD.md) wins wherever a site's look differs.
Proposed specs are in our terms. Sizes are S / M / L (cell-relative mm). Strokes are in pt. Black ink only, plus the single grey.
Limits: the searches surfaced only third-party White Rose-style material. I could not reach the official WRM, MW4K, K5 or Math-Drills pages through search, so items 3 and 4 rely partly on the general conventions of those materials (marked *conv*). Verify them against the logged-in sites before a build.

## 1. Fact family / number family
**Content model**
- **Part-whole (WRM, Y1-2):** a whole circle on top, with two part circles below joined by straight spokes. The pupil completes a missing circle and then the "eight facts" (4 additions, 4 subtractions, both orders, `=` on either side at Y2).
- **Bar model twin:** a whole bar over two part bars of proportional length.
- **Fact triangle (US):** the largest number (sum/product) sits at the TOP vertex, the two parts at the base corners, and the operation signs in the centre.
- **Fact-family house:** the three numbers sit in the roof triangle. The body has 4 ruled lines (2 add, 2 subtract) or a pre-structured `□ + □ = □`.
- **Is-this-a-fact-family items:** a set of 4 sentences or a trio, answered by circling yes/no (or ticking a box), sometimes with "why" sentence starters.

**Quality markers:** the whole is always in the same place (top), and the circles are equal size. Parts are not ordered by size. Each fact sits on its own pre-drawn frame, so the pupil writes digits only and never whole sentences.

**Spec suggestion**
- Part-whole circles: diameter 16/20/24 mm (S/M/L), stroke 1.5 pt, spokes 1 pt.
- Gap between the whole and the parts: 0.6 × diameter. Digits at our big-digit token, centred.
- Missing value: an empty circle (the circle itself is the slot). Never add a box inside the circle.
- Triangle: equilateral, side 34/42/50 mm, numbers just inside the vertices, sign centred.
- House: roof plus 4 frame rows `□ ○ □ = □`. Boxes are our standard answer slot, with the operator either printed or in an ○ the pupil fills.
- Yes/No: two words in a bank, `Yes   No`, which the pupil circles. Draw it with ≥ 12 mm between the words and a 1 pt ring in the key.

## 2. K-2 single-step word problem answer area
**Content model**
- The story is 1-3 short lines.
- Below it sits an optional bar / part-whole (WRM puts the model under the story).
- Then a number sentence frame `□ ○ □ = □` and an answer line `Answer: □ unit-word`, with the unit printed.
- Work space is a small blank area (about 1/3 of the cell). No column grid for 1-digit sums: a column grid appears only when regrouping is taught (2-digit and up).
- RUCSAC/CUBES appear as teacher posters, not in the answer area.

**Spec suggestion**
- Story at the body size with ≤ 3 lines.
- Model zone 18/22/26 mm high.
- Sentence frame: boxes 12/14/16 mm square, ○ 9/10/12 mm.
- Final answer slot: a boxed answer with the printed unit to its right (e.g. `□ apples`).
- No grid when operands < 10 and there is no regrouping.

## 3. Representations
- **Bar model:** rectangles 1.5 pt, lengths proportional within a cell. Compare models are left-aligned, with the difference shown as a dashed (grey) segment and a brace or bracket. The whole label sits above (bracket) or beside it; part labels are centred inside. The unknown is shown as `?`, or as our slot inside the bar. Height 9/11/13 mm.
- **Number line:**
  - Marked line: axis 1.5 pt with an arrowhead only when it continues. Major ticks 1 pt × 4 mm, minor 0.75 pt × 2.5 mm. Labels sit below. Equal intervals always.
  - Open line: no ticks, jumps drawn as arcs ABOVE (1 pt) with the jump size labelled above each arc.
  - Answer: a box below the target tick, or an arrow pointing to the tick with a box.
- **Ten frame:** 2×5 cells, square 10/12/14 mm, outer 1.5 pt and inner 1 pt. Counters are filled black discs of 70 % of the cell, filled left to right along the top row first. A double ten frame stacks 2 frames with a 4 mm gap.
- **Base-10:** a rod is 10 units joined, a flat is 10×10. Units are 3/3.5/4 mm, outlined 0.75 pt, unfilled or grey. Group by place left to right (hundreds, tens, ones) with gaps between groups. *conv*
- **Arrays:** rows × columns of equal dots (diameter 4/5/6 mm, gaps of 1.5 × diameter). Label as rows by columns. Never draw a ragged last row.
- **Fraction bars:** an equal-part rectangle 1.5 pt outer, 1 pt dividers, shading in the single grey. Stack bars aligned to the same whole width.
- **Tape diagram:** same as the bar model, with units drawn as equal subdivided sections.

## 4. Answer-area conventions
- Use boxes for single numbers and digits. Lines appear in the materials only for words or sentences, and we avoid those.
- Units are printed beside the box, not written by the pupil.
- Yes/no and true/false are answered by circling a printed word. Sorts use tick boxes in a 2-column table or "write the letter in the box".
- Space: one item gets a generous cell. The answer area sits on the right or at the bottom, always in the same place across items, and the key fills the same slot.

## Anti-patterns these materials avoid
- Disproportionate bars (a part bar longer than the whole).
- Uneven tick spacing.
- Decorative clip-art competing with the model.
- The answer embedded in the picture (counters revealing the sum).
- A column grid for 1-digit facts.
- Free-write lines where a box would do.
- Mixed stroke weights within one model.
- Labels touching strokes.
- Several unknowns in one K-1 item.
- The yes/no answered by writing the word.
