## 2. Problem types and progression

*No premium logins this round. From knowledge of the reference sites: MathWorksheets4Kids "multiplicative comparison" pages (write the comparison statement for an equation; write the equation for a statement; a bar model of n equal parts beside one part), K5 Learning grade 4 "multiplicative comparison" worksheets (fill the blank in "__ is 4 times as many as 6"; match the equation to the sentence), IXL grade 4 "Multiplicative comparison" / "Comparison word problems" / "Write multiplication equations for comparison statements" (pick the sentence that matches 35 = 5 x 7, choose or write the equation for a sentence, the "times as many" bar). The sites deal 10-20 items a page, multiple choice on screen; we keep 4-6 boxed cells, the same check boxes on paper and screen (no MC on screen only), and one written response per cell.*

**Problem types**

- Read the equation (task read): 35 = 5 x 7 at digit size; three sentences each with a check box: "35 is 5 times as many as 7.", "35 is 7 times as many as 5.", and one false sentence from the misconceptions ("35 is 5 more than 7.", "7 is 5 times as many as 35."). Either one or two sentences are true (dealt), positions dealt (L10). "Which sentence matches the equation?" (the build list's third type) is this task with one true sentence.
- Write the equation (task write): the sentence "24 is 3 times as many as 8." and the frame [ ] = [ ] x [ ] (three number boxes, x and = printed). 24 = 3 x 8 and 24 = 8 x 3 are both right on screen and in the key note.
- Fill the blank (task blank): "[ ] is 4 times as many as 6." one number box in the sentence; the unknown is always the larger amount (the product). The unknown multiplier and the smaller amount are `comparison_unknown`, not here.
- Story form unchanged (form story, the default): the three-unknown times-as-many stories on the word-work cell.

**Progression** (one new thing per step, P-1)

1. O2 number size (`facts`): facts to 5 x 5 (both factors 2-5) -> facts to 10 x 10 (2-10). Factor 1 is never dealt (1 times as many is not a comparison), 2 x 2 never (r5 D-A rule kept).
2. O3 support (`wpBar`, on the statements form): the scaling bar drawn and labelled -> no bar. On this form O3 and the build list's O6 "bars / plain" are the same control: mult_comparison opens with the bar, mult_comparison_plain without; one control, not two (L8 reason).
3. Task order inside the lesson (one change a step, P-1 `format`): read (the equation given, judge sentences) -> blank (one number in the sentence) -> write (the whole equation). Each task is its own page; a page never mixes tasks.
4. Structural scaffolds on every item and every rung: the x and = printed in the write frame, the check boxes, the boxed cell. Hint scaffolds that fade: the bar and its labels.

## 6. Misconceptions

For answer keys, `wrongAnswer`, error analysis, True or False? and Reason It (P-TH-13).

- **M-T1** reads the comparison as addition (additive comparison) — e.g. checks "35 is 5 more than 7"; writes 24 = 3 + 8; fills "10 is 4 times as many as 6"
- **M-T2** believes only one of the two readings is true — e.g. checks "35 is 5 times as many as 7" and leaves "35 is 7 times as many as 5" empty
- **M-T3** makes the smaller number the "times as many" — e.g. checks "7 is 5 times as many as 35"; writes 8 = 3 x 24
- **M-T4** writes the numbers in the order they are read, not where they belong — e.g. "24 is 3 times as many as 8" written 24 x 3 = 8 or 3 = 24 x 8
