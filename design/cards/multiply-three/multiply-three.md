**Problem types**

- solve (default): '(2 × 5) × 7 = [ ] × 7 = ___' as a three-line chain, the easy pair bracketed (mtGroup printed); write the inner product (working) and the product (graded).
- choose (mtBracket off): '2 × 5 × 7' then '= [ ] × [ ]' then '= ___': the pupil writes the inner product and the factor left over, so the chosen pair shows in the work (no ring-a-pair choice: the work is the production, SL-7).
- both (mtForm 'both', the associative property shown): two chains side by side, '(2 × 3) × 4' and '2 × (3 × 4)', each with its inner box and its product; two graded answers; the key writes the same product twice.
- missing (mtForm 'missing'): '3 × [ ] × 2 = 30' or '(4 × 2) × [ ] = 40' - one box, the unknown factor (2-9); products within the band.
- equality true or false? (true-false / reason-it pages only, from the provider): '(3 × 5) × 2 = 3 × (5 × 2)' true; '(3 × 5) × 2 = 3 × 5 + 2' false.

**Progression** (one new thing per step, P-1)

3. Structural (every item, every step): the chain's lines with '=' in one column, the inner-product box, the answer line. Hint (fades first): the printed bracket; on Guided the bracket is traced grey on the tries before it goes (INK-3).

## 6. Misconceptions

For answer keys, `wrongAnswer`, error analysis, True or False? and Reason It (P-TH-13).

- **M-MT1** adds the third number instead of multiplying (mt-add-last) — e.g. (2 × 5) × 7: writes 10 + 7 = 17
