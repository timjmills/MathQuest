## 2. Problem types and progression

*From repo sources: design/catalogue/vocabulary.md (the family audit: ladders VC-R / VC-P, the defects of today's six-variant mix), the owner's design in IMPROVEMENT_PLAN, the owner's 10-17 letter change (design/lesson-planner/wip-2026-10-17/vocab-letters.uncommitted.patch, superseded by the 10-25 design: the PICTURES are lettered and the box is beside the WORD), data/standards/ccss-math.json for the word sets, the detailed lessons' own vocabulary. MathWorksheets4Kids / IXL not opened this round (budget; the owner's design is explicit and outranks them): their vocabulary pages are picture-word matching with lines and a word bank, the same grammar in a denser layout.*

**Problem types**

- Match 3 pictures (vocab-match3): three pictures in a row lettered A, B, C; under them the three words in another order (never a word under its own picture), each with one letter box; the pupil writes the picture's letter in each box. Screen: the same three letter boxes, one character each (type the letter): the response is the same, never multiple choice.
- 1 picture, 3 words (vocab-pick3): one picture, a bank of three words in a thin box under it, a writing line; the pupil copies the right word onto the line. Screen: the three words are buttons; a tap drops the word onto the line (the cell's :has() rule), one graded answer.
- The lesson vocabulary page (lesson part vocab-page, a lesson whose record names its vocabSet): band 'Match the pictures:' two Match 3 cells (all six words), band 'Name the picture:' 1-picture cells for the core words (K-1 and grades 2-3: 2, grades 4-5: 4); key on the facsimile page.

**Progression** (one new thing per step, P-1)

1. Match 3 pictures (receptive: recognise the word; three pairs, the picture labelled) -> 1 picture, 3 words (productive: choose and write the word). One change a step (P-1): the response changes from a letter to a word.
2. One lesson's six words (vocabSet) -> every word of the grade's lessons (vocabGrade): the pool grows, the task does not change.
3. A page uses every word of its pool once before any word comes back; no item offers two words whose pictures could be read the same (notWith), so every item has one right answer.
