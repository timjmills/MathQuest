# Spec card: Draw the cuts: partition a shape into equal areas (template `partition`, build-list `partition_draw`)

Generated by `node tests/scripts/ws-spec-card.mjs --template partition` from `design/cards/partition/card.json` and the repo. Do not edit by hand: edit card.json and regenerate. Builders read this card; critics keep reading the full standards and RUBRIC.

A shape (rectangle, square, circle or regular hexagon) with no partition lines, on a grid, a dot lattice or edge guide dots only. The pupil draws the cuts that make n equal-area parts and writes the unit fraction of one part in a fraction slot. Equal AREA, not equal shape: on the grid a quarter of a 6 x 4 rectangle may be an L or a step, and the squares make that countable. The drawing is the production; the written slot (1/n) is the graded answer on paper; on screen both the cuts and the fraction are checked. The key draws one correct partition and says other equal cuts are right too. The same template, with the cuts printed, also draws the skill's two existing tasks (how many equal parts; what fraction is shaded), migrated off the legacy colour SVG in the same build (open question 1).

## 1. What it teaches

Partition a shape into parts with equal areas and express the area of each part as a unit fraction of the whole (3.G.A.2; M.EE.3.G.2 "recognize shapes that have been partitioned into equal areas", at target "when given a shape, cut the shape into equal areas"). Builds on 1.G.A.3 / 2.G.A.3 (halves, thirds, fourths of circles and rectangles; equal shares need not have the same shape), which the skill already teaches. Lesson fed: G3 "Partition shapes into equal areas" (design/lessons-first/BUILD_QUEUE.md row 11, lesson G3-Y4.B7, overdue).

## 2. Problem types and progression

*No premium logins this round. From knowledge of the reference sites: MathWorksheets4Kids "partitioning shapes" / "equal parts" pages (draw lines to split rectangles, circles and squares into 2, 3, 4 equal parts; partition on a grid; identify equal and unequal parts), K5 Learning grade 2-3 "fractions of shapes" / "equal parts" worksheets (divide shapes into halves, thirds, quarters; shade and name a part), IXL grade 3 "Equal parts" and "Fractions of shapes" skills (is the shape divided into equal parts?; which shape is divided into fourths?; on screen IXL mostly chooses among drawn partitions, it rarely lets the pupil draw). Repo sources: WORKSHEET_DESIGN_STANDARD RP-93 (partition items: guide dots, never the lines), RP-120 (0.75 pt outline on a shape to partition), PROBLEM_TYPES FR-06 / RM-18 (partition: tap two guide dots to add a cut) / RL-19 (circles carry 12 or 24 guide dots). The sites deal 6-12 shapes a page with free-hand cuts and no area support; we deal 4-6 boxed cells, give the grid first (equal area made countable by squares), fade it to dots and then edge dots, and keep production on screen (RM-18) where IXL turns it into multiple choice.*

**Problem types**

- Draw the cuts (new, forms=2): "Cut the rectangle into 4 equal parts." A 6 x 4 rectangle (or 4 x 4 square, circle, hexagon) with no partition lines; the pupil draws the cuts and writes "Each part is [1]/[4] of the shape." in two fraction boxes.
- Two ways (draw, Stretch and Reason It, and one item in four on Independent at support=grid): two copies of the same shape side by side, "Cut each one into 4 equal parts. Make the parts a different shape each time." One fraction slot for the pair.
- How many equal parts? (forms=0, existing, migrated): the shape drawn with its cuts; write the number of parts.
- What fraction is shaded? (forms=1, existing, migrated): the shape drawn with its cuts, k parts grey; write the fraction.
- Out of scope here: "are these parts equal in area?" (judge, Yes / No on a grid) belongs to build-list fraction_parts (2.G.A.3, M.EE.3.G.2 "recognise equal areas"); this card's True or False? and Error Analysis pages already ask it of a drawn partition.

**Progression** (one new thing per step, P-1)

1. O2 parts: halves and quarters -> thirds and sixths -> eighths (the existing "Equal parts" option, values Halves / Thirds / Fourths, gains Sixths and Eighths). Each shape deals only the n it can cut fairly on its guides: rectangle 6 x 4 (2, 3, 4, 6, 8), square 4 x 4 (2, 4, 8), circle 24 rim dots (2, 3, 4, 6, 8), hexagon (2, 3, 6).
2. O3 support (new option, rectangles and squares): grid (unit squares drawn under the shape: count the squares in each part) -> dots (the same lattice as dots, no lines) -> edge (only the guide dots on the outline). Circles and hexagons always carry their guide dots (RP-93) and take no support value.
3. O6 shape (new option): rectangles and squares -> circles -> hexagons (a page of one shape, or mixed).
4. One change a step (P-1): the parts count, the support and the shape are three controls; a ladder step changes one. The response (draw the cuts, write 1/n) never changes.

**Sizes S / M / L:** Grid pitch (one unit square) 7 / 8 / 9 mm at S / M / L (RP-5: a part of the thinnest legal shape, 1 square wide, is >= 6 mm across at S). Rectangle 6 x 4 units = 42 x 28 / 48 x 32 / 54 x 36 mm; square 4 x 4 = 28 / 32 / 36 mm. Circle diameter 36 / 42 / 50 mm (frac-model DIM, section 11.2), 24 rim guide dots (every 15 deg, the first at 12 o'clock) and a centre dot; an eighth is 7.1 mm across at mid-radius at S (RP-93 >= 6). Regular hexagon 40 / 46 / 54 mm vertex to vertex (side 20 / 23 / 27 mm), guide dots at the 6 vertices, the 6 edge midpoints and the centre. Fraction slot: two digit boxes and a 1.5 pt bar at the working digit size (TY-10, 16 / 22 / 28 pt), beside the shape when the cell is >= 55 mm wide past it, else under it. Cell: one shape + its slot, 2 columns at S / M / L for rectangles, squares and circles (cell width <= 93 mm), 2 columns for a hexagon at S / M, 1 at L if the measured cell is over 93 mm; a "two ways" item is one cell a row.

- Ink (INK-10 set only): the outline of the shape to cut 0.75 pt (RP-120), no fill; grid lines inside the shape 0.5 pt black (a line inside a visual that never bounds a writing place); lattice dots 0.8 mm solid black, rim / vertex / centre guide dots 1.2 mm solid black (INK-5 anchor dots); no grey on the pupil page. The grid stops at the outline; nothing is drawn outside the shape.
- Never draw the answer (RP-1, L3): no partition line on the pupil page; the grid, dots and the shape are the same on every item of a section whatever n is (a 6 x 4 rectangle carries every n it can deal), so neither the lattice nor the dot count tells n. n is printed in the instruction (3.G.A.2 asks the pupil to cut into a given number and name the part); the fraction is the written answer.
- Key (AK-1, AK-2): the pupil's own cell with ONE correct partition drawn at 1.5 pt black (heavier than the grid and the outline so it reads over them), the fraction written bold in the pupil's boxes, and under the cell, key only, the line "Other equal cuts are right too." in the text size. A "two ways" key draws two partitions whose parts differ in shape (equal-area, non-congruent: an L or a step quarter on the 6 x 4).
- Model cell (PT-MOD-1, RP-1): the completed cuts are dotted (LS-1, 1 pt round dots at 1.2 mm), the squares of one part counted in small digits inside them ("6, 6, 6, 6"), the Say band "I cut it into 4 equal parts. Each part is one quarter, 1/4."
- Pre-cut tasks (forms 0 / 1, migrated): the same shape with its cuts printed 0.75 pt (partitions, ink table), shaded parts flat grey (INK-3, hatch when photocopy-safe); outline 1.5 pt (a shape to judge, RP-120). The guide dots and grid are not drawn on pre-cut items.
- Screen (RM-18): the same drawing; tap one guide dot then another to add a straight cut between them (snaps to dots; on support=grid the grid nodes are the dots); tap a cut to remove it. Cuts are chained segments, so an L or step cut on the lattice is drawn dot to dot. The fraction boxes are data-mq-cell joined "/" (frac-model). The item is right when the cuts make exactly n faces of equal area (computed: unit squares flood-filled across cut edges on the lattice; sector angles on a circle; polygon areas on a hexagon, tolerance 1 %) AND the fraction is 1/n. Never multiple choice (a drawn production on paper stays a production on screen). The dots are >= 9 mm apart on screen at 390 px (touch target via an invisible 9 mm hit circle).
- Footprint: the cell is measured, never fixed (L1): S fits more cells a page than L (rectangles: 8 / 6 / 6 a page at S / M / L, two columns, four / three rows); the I Can and the instruction follow the option (L6, L28: "I Can cut a shape into equal parts and name one part" for forms=2).
- Distractors (wrongAnswer, error analysis / true-false): a drawn partition with n parts of UNEQUAL area (M-P2), a correct equal-area non-congruent partition offered as "wrong" for the pupil to defend (M-P1, the answer is True), and the fraction n/1 or 1/(cuts) (M-P3, M-P4). Every wrong answer differs from the right one per slot (L27).

## 6. Misconceptions

For answer keys, `wrongAnswer`, error analysis, True or False? and Reason It (P-TH-13).

- **M-P1** equal parts must be the same shape (an L-shaped quarter of a 6 x 4 rectangle is "not a quarter") — e.g. marks an equal-area, non-congruent partition "not equal"; True or False? says False
- **M-P2** counts parts that are not equal (any n pieces are n "quarters") — e.g. cuts a rectangle into 4 strips of 1, 1, 2 and 2 squares and writes 1/4
- **M-P3** counts the cuts, not the parts — e.g. draws 3 cuts for quarters and writes 1/3; or draws 4 cuts and makes 5 parts
- **M-P4** writes the fraction upside down or as the count — e.g. 4/1 or 4 for one quarter
- **M-P5** cuts a circle across, not through the centre, for thirds (parallel chords) — e.g. three slices of a circle by two parallel lines, called thirds
