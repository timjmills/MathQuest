## 1. UFLI Foundations Overview

UFLI Foundations (University of Florida Literacy Institute Foundations) is a systematic, explicit phonics program developed by Dr. Holly Lane and Dr. Valentina Contesse at the University of Florida. It is a K-2 foundational reading skills program designed for 60 minutes of instruction per lesson (split across two school days, 30 minutes each).

**128-lesson scope and sequence**, organized into 16 lesson sets:

| Set | Approx. Lessons | Focus |
|-----|-----------------|-------|
| 1 | 1-10 | Early alphabet: m, a, s, t, p, f, i, n, CVC words |
| 2 | 11-19 | Alphabet continued: b, o, g, h, j, e, w, x, y, z |
| 3 | 20-28 | Alphabet continued: c, k, ck, qu, v, short vowels, FLSZ rule |
| 4 | 29-38 | Alphabet review, longer CVC words, blends |
| 5 | 39-44 | Digraphs: sh, ch, th, wh, ph |
| 6 | 45-53 | Digraph review, ng /ŋ/, nk /ŋk/ |
| 7 | 54-62 | VCe (silent e): a_e, i_e, o_e, u_e, e_e |
| 8 | 63-68 | Suffixes: -s, -es, -ed, -ing on VCe words |
| 9 | 69-76 | Ending patterns: -all, -ild, -old, -ost, -ind, -olt |
| 10 | 77-83 | R-controlled vowels: ar, or, er, ir, ur |
| 11 | 84-90 | Long vowel teams: ai/ay, ee/ea, oa/ow |
| 12 | 91-97 | More vowel teams and diphthongs: oi/oy, ou/ow, oo, ew/ue |
| 13 | 98-105 | Silent letters and trigraphs: kn, wr, gn, dge, tch, mb |
| 14 | 106-112 | Multisyllabic words: vc/cv, v/cv, vc/v syllable division |
| 15 | 113-120 | Affixes: prefixes un-, re-, pre-, dis-, mis- |
| 16 | 121-128 | Affixes continued: suffixes -ful, -less, -ness, -tion, -sion |

**Grade-level pacing:** Kindergarten covers lessons 1-44. First grade targets lessons 38B-84 (with overlap for review). Second grade spans lessons 38B-128.

**The 8-step lesson routine** (split Day 1 / Day 2):
1. Phonemic Awareness (2-3 min) — oral blending and segmenting, no print
2. Visual Drill (5 min) — grapheme card → student says phoneme
3. Auditory Drill (5 min) — teacher says phoneme → student writes grapheme
4. Blending Drill (5 min) — blending board, word-by-word choral reading
5. New Concept (15 min Day 1; 5 min review Day 2) — explicit introduction with scripted language
6. Word Work (10 min) — word chains, sorts, letter-tile manipulation
7. Irregular Words / Heart Words (6 min) — orthographic mapping of tricky words
8. Connected Text (15 min) — decodable passage reading for fluency and comprehension

**Roll-and-Read** and **Decodable Passages** are supplementary materials that reinforce Step 8 (Connected Text). Roll-and-Read is primarily a fluency warm-up or extension. Decodable Passages are the main Step 8 text; each lesson from lesson 8 onward has one.

## 2. Roll-and-Read Format

### Visual Layout

A single landscape-oriented page with a bordered grid. The header reads "**Roll and Read**" (bold, centered) with the lesson number and skill label as a subtitle (e.g., "Lesson 52: n /ŋ/ (as in nk)").

The grid is **6 columns × 7 rows**:
- Row 1 (header): Each of the 6 columns shows a drawn dice face (1 dot through 6 dots), displayed as simple black-dot-on-white square icons inside the cell.
- Rows 2-7: Each cell contains one decodable word in a clean sans-serif font at approximately 28-32pt, centered in the cell, lower-case.

The grid has visible borders on all cells. No color is used — purely black and white. The UFLI Foundations logo appears in the bottom-left corner. Copyright line appears in the bottom-right.

Total word count per sheet: **36 words** (6 columns × 6 word rows).

### How It Is Used in the Classroom

The teacher distributes one sheet per student (or projects it). Students use a physical die or virtual die. On each roll, the student reads every word in that column top-to-bottom (rows 2-7). The activity runs for approximately 5 minutes, with students rolling 2-3 times, working with a partner or the class chorally. There is no writing component. The goal is building fluency and automaticity with the lesson's target pattern.

### Word Selection Principles

- All 36 words use the lesson's target grapheme-phoneme correspondence prominently.
- Words vary in onset complexity: some are simple VC/CVC (e.g., "ink"), others add blends or clusters (e.g., "shrink," "thrust").
- No heart words appear on Roll-and-Read sheets — every word is fully decodable using skills taught to that lesson.
- Some lessons that are review lessons (e.g., Lesson 53: digraphs review 2) include words spanning multiple previously taught patterns, mixing them deliberately across columns.
- More complex lessons (like Lesson 54, VCe) sometimes provide **two Roll-and-Read sheets** (labeled "(1)" and "(2)") for additional practice volume.

## 3. Decodable Passage Format

### Visual Layout

Portrait orientation, full page. Header area:
- Top-left: "Lesson N: [skill label]" in small plain text
- Top-right: UFLI Foundations logo
- Title: Bold, centered, approximately 20-24pt

Below the title is a labeled illustration box: "Illustrate the story here:" with a rectangular bordered box occupying roughly the top third of the page. This box is blank — students draw their own illustration after reading.

The passage text follows in two indented paragraphs (each paragraph indented ~1 em). Font is a clean serif or transitional serif (the original uses a font similar to Palatino or Georgia) at approximately 18-20pt, which is intentionally large for early readers. Line spacing is generous (approximately 1.5x). No images other than the illustration box and logo.

Copyright line at the bottom-right. No heart-word footer panel appears in the scanned samples — irregular words are embedded in the text without special markup on the student page (teachers reference a separate word list).

### Passage Specifications

| Metric | Typical Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Total word count | 90-160 words | Grows across the 128-lesson arc |
| Sentence count | 10-20 sentences | Short, punchy sentences early; compound sentences later |
| Paragraph count | 2-3 indented paragraphs | Consistent across all samples examined |
| Vocabulary control | 100% decodable + taught heart words | No word appears that uses an untaught pattern |
| Illustration box | ~1/3 page, blank bordered rectangle | Student-drawn; supports comprehension |
| Heart words used | Embedded in text without markup on student sheet | Taught separately in Step 7 |

**Lesson 51 — ng /ŋ/ — "King and Ash Sing"**
*(~145 words, 3 paragraphs)*

King and Ash are best pals. To have fun, King and Ash sing songs. King brings Ash a book of songs. "Which song should we sing?" asks King. "Let's pick a quick song and then a long song," said Ash. King and Ash sing their songs.

King's mom just got back from the shop. "Will you sing me a song?" she asks. "Yes!" yell Ash and King. They grab the book of songs. "We have to pick the best song for your mom," said Ash. King looks in the book. He sees the best song. "This is it," he says. King and Ash sing. King's mom claps and says, "I am so glad you sang that song."

---

**Lesson 52 — n /ŋ/ (as in nk) — "Stink in the Bank"**
*(~108 words, 2 paragraphs)*

Hank has a job at the bank. When Hank gets to the bank, he sniffs a bad smell. "What is that smell?" Hank thinks. "Is it a skunk?" He sniffs his desk. "No, it's not my desk." He checks the fish tank. "No, this is not what stinks." He walks to the back of the bank. When he gets to the back, he sees the small trash bin.

"Oh no!" Hank yells, "I left all the trash in the bin." Hank sniffs the trash. "Yuck!" he yells, "that's what stinks!" Hank brings the trash to the big bin and slams the lid shut. Hank is glad he got rid of the stink.

---

**Lesson 53 — digraphs review 2 — "The Ship Dock"**
*(~140 words, 3 paragraphs)*

Quin goes to the dock with Jax. They sit on the dock and look at the ships. Jax looks up and sees a big clock. "What is that clock for?" he asks. Quin says, "There is a bell in the clock. When the bell rings, the ships can go to the dock."

Quin and Jax look for ships that could go to the dock. "I think that tall black ship will go to the dock," said Quin. "I think this big pink ship will go to the dock," said Jax.

The bell rings. Ding dong. Ding dong. Which ship will go to the dock? The tall black ship goes past the dock. The big pink ship goes past the dock. "Look!" said Quin. Jax sees a small red ship. The small red ship stops at the dock. "We should have said the red ship!"

---

**Lesson 54 — a_e /ā/ — "Cave in the Maze"**
*(~116 words, 3 paragraphs)*

Dale and Jade went into a maze to win a game. The maze was long and full of snakes. Dale said to Jade, "I can not go on." "Yes, you can. You are brave!" said Jade. Dale and Jade walk and walk.

At last, they made a left and got to the end of the maze. Then they see six small caves. "Let's go into the last cave," said Jade. In the back of the cave, they see a gate! The gate is shut. How will they pass?

Dale shakes the gate. Jade kicks the gate. The gate is still shut. "Look! The gate has a lock," yells Jade. Dale twists the lock. Pop! The gate swings in. Dale and Jade win the game!

---

### Vocabulary Control Analysis (Lesson 54 example)

Every word in "Cave in the Maze" is decodable using skills taught through lesson 54:
- CVC words (all short vowels): went, can, not, got, end, six, has, win
- Blends + short vowels: last, left, full, still, lock, pass, yells
- Digraphs: shakes
- nk pattern (lesson 52): snakes (no nk, but "snakes" is VCe — fully within lesson 54 scope)
- a_e pattern (lesson 54): Dale, Jade, maze, game, made, cave, caves, gate, brave, shakes, wave *(note: "snakes" = sn + a_e + s)*
- Heart words used: into, a, to, the, of, and, said, you, are, they, then, they, in, how

No word in the passage uses a phonics pattern beyond lesson 54.

## 4. How the UFLI Lesson Plan Integrates These Materials

### Roll-and-Read (Day 2, ~5 minutes)

Used as a fluency warm-up at the start of Day 2 or as a transition activity. The teacher projects the grid or gives each student a printed copy. One student (or the teacher) rolls a physical die, and the class reads that column aloud together. On subsequent rolls, individual students or partners take turns. Target: each column read 2-3 times total across the activity. No writing, no answer checking — pure oral decoding at speed.

Roll-and-Read directly serves UFLI's automaticity goal: students encounter the target pattern in 6 words per roll, experiencing it in multiple phonological environments (different onsets, rimes, syllable structures) rapidly and repeatedly. Lesson 54's two-sheet format doubles the word volume for the VCe concept, which is one of the most foundational skill shifts in the sequence (introducing the "magic e" principle).

### Decodable Passage (Day 2, Step 8, ~15 minutes)

The passage is the culminating connected-text experience. Teachers typically use a 3-part routine:
1. **First read (teacher-led or choral):** Read the passage once together, pointing to each word. Teacher models expression and pacing.
2. **Partner read:** Students read to each other, alternating sentences or paragraphs. Partners listen and give a thumbs-up for smooth reading.
3. **Comprehension discussion:** 2-3 brief questions ("What did Hank find in the bank?" / "How did Dale and Jade get past the gate?"). No written response required at this stage.

The illustration box is typically used as a post-reading extension: students draw a scene from the story, which reinforces comprehension and serves as a comprehension accountability check without requiring writing.

### Home Practice Sheet (per-lesson, sent home)

The Home Practice sheet (confirmed via the Lesson 54 sample) is a half-page takeaway for families. It contains:
- New Concept label (e.g., "a_e") with 6 sample words
- New Irregular Words box (e.g., "who" with two black heart symbols below the tricky letters)
- Word Work Chains (e.g., make → lake → cake → bake; name → same → came → game)
- Sample Word Work Chain Script (numbered steps, alternating [spelling] and [reading] moves)
- Sentences for home practice (2 example sentences using the target pattern and new heart words)

The sheet prints two copies per page (identical, stacked) to allow the teacher to keep one for records.

## 5. Replication Strategy for Literacy Quest

### Roll-and-Read Widget

A dedicated `roll-and-read` widget is the right approach — it cannot be adequately replicated with `mc-text` because the activity is student-paced oral reading, not a question-and-answer format.

**Widget design:**
- Render a 6-column × 6-row CSS grid with visible cell borders, matching the print format exactly
- Header row shows dice face SVGs (1-6 dots) in each column — pure CSS or inline SVG, no images needed
- Word cells use a large sans-serif font (24-28px), centered, lower-case
- A "Roll Dice" button generates a random integer 1-6, then highlights that entire column (e.g., light blue background + border accent) while the other 5 columns dim slightly
- A "Read Along" mode (Stage 2): highlight each word in the active column sequentially on a timer (one word per 1.5-2 seconds) so students can track at their own pace or follow along
- `voice-memo` widget integration (Stage 3): student records themselves reading the column; teacher can play it back for fluency assessment

**Data structure per Roll-and-Read atom:**
```json
{
  "type": "roll-and-read",
  "lesson": 52,
  "skill": "n /ŋ/ (as in nk)",
  "sheet": 1,
  "columns": [
    ["bank", "think", "drank", "pink", "chunk", "plank"],
    ["blink", "stink", "blank", "bunk", "clink", "slunk"],
    ["slink", "thank", "ink", "clunk", "crank", "dunk"],
    ["link", "honk", "flunk", "sink", "hunk", "junk"],
    ["drink", "plunk", "skunk", "prank", "stunk", "sunk"],
    ["rink", "sank", "trunk", "wink", "rank", "clank"]
  ]
}
```

### Decodable Passage Widget

The existing `passage-render.js` handles this correctly. Decodable passages map directly to the `passage-mc-set` and `passage-hot-text` widget types already in Literacy Quest's Wave 2.5 atom stubs.

**Passage atom data structure:**
```json
{
  "type": "passage-render",
  "lesson": 52,
  "skill": "n /ŋ/ (as in nk)",
  "title": "Stink in the Bank",
  "text": "Hank has a job at the bank...",
  "heartWords": ["the", "his", "what", "no", "oh", "all"],
  "targetWords": ["bank", "thinks", "skunk", "tank", "stinks", "brings", "stink"],
  "illustrationPrompt": "Draw what Hank found in the back of the bank."
}
```

The `illustrationPrompt` field can power a drawing canvas widget (Stage 3) or simply display as a text prompt for offline drawing.

**Follow-up comprehension questions** (2-3 per passage) use the existing `passage-mc-set` format and are authored as part of the same lesson bundle.

## 6. Implications for Literacy Quest

- **Build the `roll-and-read` widget in Wave 5.** It needs: a 6×6 CSS grid, dice-face SVG headers, column-highlight logic on roll, and a JSON-driven word list. Estimated scope: one day of frontend work. It is a high-visibility widget because teachers recognize it immediately.

- **Author ~20 Roll-and-Read atoms covering the 16 UFLI lesson sets,** prioritizing one per set boundary (e.g., lessons 10, 19, 28, 38, 44, 53, 62, 68, 76, 83, 90, 97, 105, 112, 120, 128). For sets with a two-sheet print original (like Lesson 54), include both sheets as separate atoms linked to the same lesson.

- **Author ~30 decodable-passage atoms** at a density of roughly one per 4 lessons (every lesson-set midpoint and boundary), giving coverage across the full 128-lesson arc. The full UFLI Foundations Decodable Passages PDF (`UFLI-Foundations-Decodables-ALL.pdf`) is the canonical source and must be licensed or used only as a structural model for original writing.

- **Curated content authoring:** The Roll-and-Read word lists and passage texts are CC-BY-NC (University of Florida Literacy Institute). For Literacy Quest to ship these as in-app content, either (a) obtain a use license from UFLI directly (they offer a free teacher toolbox), or (b) author original decodable passages that follow the same vocabulary-control rules but use different characters and plots. Original authoring is the safer long-term path. The word lists for Roll-and-Read (purely phonics-pattern-controlled common words) carry minimal copyright concern and can likely be reproduced under fair use for educational use.

- **Build a "UFLI Lesson N" launcher** (Wave 6 or later) that chains Roll-and-Read → Decodable Passage → Comprehension MC → Spelling/Word-Chain Practice into a single 30-minute session bundle. This maps directly to UFLI Day 2 (Steps 6-8) and gives teachers a one-click digital version of their lesson close.

- **Home Practice sheet integration:** The Home Practice format (word chains, sample sentences, heart words) maps well to existing Literacy Quest widget types: word-chain is a sequenced `word-build` or `mc-text` activity; sentences map to `passage-hot-text`; heart words map to a simple `flashcard` widget. The full Home Practice experience could be a lightweight 3-widget bundle sent home as a shareable link.

- **Heart word / irregular word handling:** In the Roll-and-Read widget, all words are fully decodable — no special markup needed. In passage atoms, tag heart words with a `heartWords` array so the renderer can optionally underline or highlight the "tricky" part (e.g., underline the "o" in "who" since /h/ and /u/ are regular but the vowel spelling is irregular). This matches UFLI's orthographic mapping approach.

- **Illustration box for decodable passages:** Render a blank dashed-border box below the passage title with the prompt "Illustrate the story here." For digital use, this can be a simple tap-to-open drawing canvas (Stage 3 feature) or just display the text prompt so students draw on paper alongside a tablet. Either way, preserve this structural element — it is pedagogically significant (comprehension check without writing burden for early readers).

- **Two-sheet lessons (e.g., Lesson 54):** Store both sheets as sibling atoms under the same lesson node in the content tree. The launcher UI should surface them as "Sheet 1" and "Sheet 2" with a clear progression indicator. Teachers may choose to use one or both depending on time.

- **Scope and sequence alignment tag:** Every Roll-and-Read and Decodable Passage atom should carry `ufliLesson`, `ufliSet`, and `ufliSkill` fields. This enables future features: a teacher can select "I am on Lesson 52" and Literacy Quest surfaces all matching Roll-and-Read, Decodable Passage, and Home Practice atoms for that exact lesson without manual searching.
