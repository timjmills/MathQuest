## 2. Sampled Content Per Book

### Read & Understand with Leveled Texts, Grade K (Evan-Moor EMC 3440) — 166 pages

**Page format:** Each story unit = 6 pages: (1) 2-page reading strip (large print, 3–5 sentences), (2) picture dictionary, (3) recall/cut-and-glue activity, (4) word/number skill activity, (5) phonics same-sound activity, (6) critical thinking / drawing activity.

**Story length and complexity:** 3–5 sentences, ~20–40 words, guided reading levels A–C. No Lexile assigned at this level. Vocabulary is 3–4 illustrated words per story (picture dictionary).

**Question types:** Cut-and-glue recall, circle-the-word, mark-the-picture, connect-the-dots, draw-the-answer. No text answer input — all visual/motor. Not auto-gradable in a digital context unless adapted to click-to-select.

**Auto-gradable potential:** Cut-and-glue and circle activities map cleanly to click-to-select MC or picture-match in a digital format.

**Sample stories (pages 9–50 range):** "One or Two?" (singular/plural), "My Cat" (actions: jump/play/sleep), "Up and Down" (directional words), "Can You?" (action verbs), "Animal Babies," "In the Car," "Count the Pups."

---

### Read & Understand with Leveled Texts, Grade 1 (Evan-Moor EMC 3441) — 166 pages

**Page format:** Same 6-page unit structure as Grade K. Adds a word-family activity slot (e.g., –un, –ock families) and a dot-to-dot or matching activity.

**Story length and complexity:** 4–8 sentences, ~40–80 words, guided reading levels C–H. Stories use a single scene with dialogue.

**Question types:** Draw-the-answer (recall 3 items from story), word family fill-in (b+un=___, s+un=___), initial-sound identification (color pictures that begin with /b/), matching (who owns the shoes?). Not MC at this level.

**Auto-gradable potential:** Word family fill-in and initial-sound activities adapt directly to fill-in-the-blank and click-to-select formats.

**Sample stories:** "In My Bed" (bunny/blanket/book), "What Will I Take?" (packing for Grandma's), "Turn Off the TV" (Mom/Gramps/Addy family), "The Campfire" (nature sounds), "Sparky" (pet dog).

### Read & Understand with Leveled Texts, Grade 2 (Evan-Moor EMC 3442) — 166 pages, 21 stories, Lexile 330–480

**Page format:** 8 pages per story: reading passage (1 page, ~150–250 words), 8 written-response comprehension questions, sequencing cut-and-glue, vocabulary/word work, compare-contrast, and a "Comparing Texts" cross-story connection page (new in this edition).

**Story length and complexity:** ~150–250 words, Lexile 330–480. Mixed fiction and nonfiction. Stories use 2–4 paragraphs with dialogue.

**Question types:** 8 written comprehension questions (recall + inference). Sequencing (cut-and-glue). Vocabulary in context. Some compare-contrast. The written-question format requires open-ended sentence answers — not auto-gradable as-is, but literal recall questions (#1–5) can be adapted to MC.

**Auto-gradable potential:** Questions 1–5 (recall) adapt to MC; questions 6–8 (inference/application) adapt to short-answer with model answers.

**Sample stories:** "Max and the Funny Fox" (~180 words, fiction, camping/nature), "Bugs!", "The Giant Carrot," "The Three Billy Goats Gruff," "Frogs," "Ducks Don't Get Wet," "Penguins."

### Daily Reading Comprehension, Grade 1 (Evan-Moor EMC 3451) — 198 pages

**Page format:** 5-day weekly format. Teacher page (1 page) + 5 student pages. Each student page: title, 3–5 sentence passage (40–70 words), 3 MC questions (fill-in-the-circle), 1 phonics/sight word/vocabulary task.

**Story length and complexity:** 40–70 words, approx. Lexile 100–200. Simple subject-predicate sentences, rhyming stories ("Oh, my! I see a pig in the sky."), animal and family topics.

**Question types:** All 3 comprehension questions are fill-in-the-circle MC (3 options). The language development task varies: beginning sounds (circle the word), vowel sounds, rhyming words. Entirely auto-gradable.

**Skills covered (30 weekly units):** Main idea, who and what, sequence, cause and effect, compare/contrast, make inferences, prediction, character and setting, nonfiction text features, author's purpose.

**Sample passages:** "This bug can hop/run/walk/fly" (main idea: how bugs move), "The cat is on my hat" (main idea: cat sat on hat), "I see a pig in the sky" (fantasy), "The mother fox is in a den" (informational: fox kits), "Mop is my little dog" (character detail).

### Daily Reading Comprehension, Grade 3 (Evan-Moor EMC 3453) — 198 pages

**Page format:** Same 5-day structure. Teacher page + 5 student pages. Each student page: passage (~100–200 words, fiction or nonfiction), strategy practice activity (visualization, connections, organization, etc.), skill practice MC questions.

**Story length and complexity:** ~100–200 words, Lexile ~400–600. Two to four paragraphs. Includes descriptive language, adjectives, subject-specific vocabulary.

**Question types:** Strategy practice (open-ended: "circle the words that helped you visualize") + 3–4 MC skill questions. Partially auto-gradable (MC), partially open-ended.

**Skills/strategies covered:** Visualization, make connections, organization, determine important information, ask questions, monitor comprehension; main idea/details, sequence, cause/effect, fact/opinion, compare/contrast, inferences, prediction, character/setting, fantasy vs. reality, author's purpose, nonfiction text features, visual information.

**Sample passages:** "Ways Dogs Talk" (dog body language, informational ~120 words), penguin-vs.-puffin comparison, puffin fuzzy creature description, story about Fabio, "The Mitten" retell.

### Building Spelling Skills, Daily Practice Grade 2 (Evan-Moor EMC 2706) — 166 pages

**Page format:** 30 weekly units. Each unit = 5 pages: (1) Spelling List (read-copy-cover-spell), (2) Find the Mistakes + Visual Memory boxes, (3) Dictation sentences + Word Meaning fill-in, (4) Word Study (sort, rhyme, change-a-letter), (5) Bonus activities (crossword, word search, or writing). Plus take-home lists, class record sheet, and student dictionary template.

**Story length:** No passages. Pure spelling drill. 10 words/week + 2 bonus words. Words tied to weekly phonics focus.

**Question types:** Read-and-spell self-check, misspelled word identification (mark X), visual memory grid fill-in, multiple-choice word meaning (choose from 3 options), word sort (short a/e/i/o/u columns), rhyming pair match, change-one-letter to make a new word, dictation sentences. Most activities are fill-in or select — auto-gradable.

**Phonics scope (Grade 2, 30 weeks):** Short vowels (Wks 1–3), CVC/CVCC patterns, –all/–and families, long vowels (silent-e), r-controlled, diphthongs (oi/oy, ou/ow), /oo/ sounds, homophones, contractions, plural inflections, prefixes (re-, un-), compound words, high-frequency words, word families.

### Spectrum Spelling Grade 3 (Carson-Dellosa) — 196 pages

**Page format:** 22 lessons + 5 reviews. Each lesson = 4 pages: (1) Spelling Tips + write-the-word, (2) Words in Context fill-in passage + Word Building, (3) Fun with Words, (4) Words Across the Curriculum + Dictionary Practice. Review pages consolidate every 5 lessons.

**Story length:** Words in Context uses a 8–12 line cloze passage (~80–120 words) built around the lesson's spelling list. Students fill in 10 blanks from the spelling words.

**Question types:** Write-the-word (dictated), fill-in-the-blank cloze, alphabetical-order sorting, dictionary guide-word practice, irregular verb matching (present → past tense). Mostly auto-gradable.

**Lesson scope (Grade 3):** Short a/o, short e/i/u, long a/o, long e/i, /ü/ sound, final double consonants, /el/ sound, double consonants, –ed/–ing endings, /oi/, /ou/, /ô/, homophones, contractions, easily misspelled words, spl/spr/str blends, /s/ and /j/ sounds, plurals, days/months/holidays, compound words, possessives, syllables, antonyms/synonyms.

### Spectrum Reading Grade 4 (Carson-Dellosa) — 180 pages

**Page format:** 70 paired lessons. Each pair = (1) fiction or nonfiction passage (~400–600 words, 9–13 numbered paragraphs) + (2) questions page (6–11 items). Covers 35 fiction/35 nonfiction topics across thematic units. Answer key at back.

**Story length and complexity:** ~400–600 words, Lexile approximately 650–850. Prose uses numbered paragraphs, natural dialogue, descriptive language. Each story stands alone.

**Question types:** T/F statements, character-attribution (write C or G), written short-answer (1 sentence), multiple-choice implicit from text. Mix of auto-gradable (T/F, MC) and open-ended (short-answer). Approximately 60% auto-gradable per page.

**Sample passages:** "A Morning in Maine" / "Grandpa's Light Show" / "The Land of the Northern Lights" (thematic trio about Maine + northern lights), "Monkey Training," "Soccer Summer," "The Peregrine Project," "Mars Mission," "Pony Express Riders," "Lone Star Camels."

### Spectrum Writing Workbook Grade 2 (Carson-Dellosa) — 116 pages

**Page format:** 3 chapters: Writing a Story (14 lessons), Writing to Inform (12 lessons), Writing an Opinion (5 lessons). Each lesson = 2–3 pages of guided writing practice with sentence starters, word webs, and scaffolded drafting. Plus a Writer's Handbook and Answer Key.

**Content structure:** Model sentence → student extends/rewrites → prewriting web → draft sentences → peer/adult review checklist. Not passage-based; entirely student-generative.

**Question types / activities:** Complete the describing word, fill in the web (sensory details), write sentences answering guided questions (What did you see? What did you hear?), fact vs. opinion sort, book review draft, opinion letter following writing process. Mostly open-ended.

**Auto-gradable potential:** Low (writing is generative). However, word webs, fact/opinion sorts, and sentence-starters adapt to structured prompts in a digital context.

### Cold Reads for Fluency and Comprehension, Grade 3 (Savvas myView) — 196 pages

**Page format:** 5 units × 5 weeks = 25 cold reads per level, 3 levels per week (Developing, On-Level, Advanced). Each read = independent passage (~150–350 words) + 4–5 comprehension questions at DOK 1–3. Teacher's manual includes item analysis charts, fluency scoring protocols, individual/class fluency progress charts, and answer keys.

**Story length and complexity:** Developing level ~150 words; On-Level ~200–250 words; Advanced ~300–350 words. All are unseen ("cold") texts. Fiction and informational, mapped to CCSS standards.

**Question types:** All MC (4 options, A–D). DOK 1: locate information. DOK 2: analyze text structure, identify main idea, explain author's purpose, distinguish viewpoint. DOK 3: evaluate, synthesize, infer theme. Entirely auto-gradable.

**Skills covered (5 units × 5 weeks):** Analyze text structure, identify main idea and key details, explain author's purpose, distinguish viewpoint, identify play elements, analyze text features, analyze point of view, infer theme. Mapped to RL/RI 3.1–3.7.

**Sample question passages observed:** Ocean sounds / whale/dolphin communication (informational, ~200 words), solar system planets (informational), mallard duck behavior (informational), dog body language (informational). All written at clean Lexile-appropriate prose.

## 4. Pedagogical Patterns to Extract

These are the structural and instructional conventions shared across all sampled workbooks that should directly inform Literacy Quest's content authoring guidelines:

**Passage structure (universal):** Every passage begins with a title + small illustration (or avatar in digital). The passage body is 1–4 short paragraphs. A picture dictionary or vocabulary box appears before or alongside the passage in K–2, replaced by bolded in-text vocabulary in Grades 3+.

**Passage length by grade:**
- K: 20–40 words (3–5 sentences)
- Grade 1: 40–80 words (4–10 sentences, simple paragraphs)
- Grade 2: 120–200 words (2–4 paragraphs)
- Grade 3: 150–300 words (3–5 paragraphs, descriptive language)
- Grade 4: 350–600 words (5–12 numbered paragraphs, dialogue)

**Question count and types per passage (universal):**
- K: 2–3 picture-select or circle-the-word (recall only)
- Grade 1: 3 MC (3-option, fill-in-the-circle); 1 phonics/language task
- Grade 2: 6–8 questions (literal recall + 1–2 inference); mix of MC and written
- Grade 3: 4–5 MC questions (DOK 1–3); plus 1 strategy practice (open-ended)
- Grade 4: 5–11 questions (T/F, MC, short-answer); 60% auto-gradable

**Comprehension question taxonomy (all grades):** Every passage produces questions across at least 3 of these types:
1. Literal recall (find it in the text)
2. Vocabulary in context (what does ___ mean?)
3. Main idea / central message
4. Sequence (what happened first/next/last?)
5. Cause and effect
6. Inference / drawing conclusions
7. Author's purpose
8. Compare and contrast
9. Character / setting identification

**Vocabulary control:** K–1 passages use only high-frequency sight words + 3–5 picture-dictionary words. Grade 2 passages use Lexile-appropriate language with 2–3 "stretch" words. Grade 3+ introduces topic-specific vocabulary (e.g., "bioluminescent," "migration") with context clues embedded in the passage.

**Spelling pattern structure (Building Spelling Skills + Spectrum Spelling):** Each week has a phonics focus (e.g., short vowel –at family, long-a silent-e). 10 words/week + 2 bonus. Daily activity types: read/copy/cover-spell, visual memory, word meaning (MC), word sort, change-a-letter, dictation. Weeks build cumulatively.

**Writing structure (Spectrum Writing):** Every writing unit follows: (1) model sentence/paragraph, (2) guided practice with sentence starters, (3) student draft with structured questions, (4) peer/adult review checklist. The three macro-genres (narrative, informational, opinion) each receive an entire chapter with 10–14 lessons.

**Reading fluency model (Cold Reads):** Three differentiated levels per week (Developing / On-Level / Advanced) allow the same weekly skill to be practiced across a reading range of ~200 Lexile points. All items are MC, all aligned to specific CCSS standards, and all are scored with a words-correct-per-minute fluency rubric.

## 6. Implications for Literacy Quest

- **Target ~50 literary + ~50 informational AI-generated passages per grade level (K–4)** for the comprehension strands, using the workbooks above as structural templates. That is ~500 total passages across five grades. Model passage length, vocabulary level, and question distribution after the grade-band norms described in Section 4. Prioritize Grades 1–3 first, as those have the densest reference material.

- **Each passage object should follow a standard schema:** `{ id, title, grade, lexile_band, word_count, genre: "literary"|"informational", skill_focus, paragraphs: string[], vocabulary: [{word, definition}], questions: [{type, stem, options?, answer, dok_level}] }`. The question array should include 4–6 items covering at least 3 distinct comprehension question types per passage.

- **Generate ~30 spelling units per grade (Grades 1–4)** covering the phonics-pattern scope documented in Section 4, modeled after Building Spelling Skills Daily Practice and Spectrum Spelling. Each unit object: `{ grade, week, phonics_focus, word_list: string[], bonus_words: string[], activities: [{type, content}] }`. Activity types to include: word-sort, fill-in-meaning (MC), change-a-letter, cloze-dictation.

- **Generate ~20 writing scaffold atoms per grade (Grades 1–4)** covering the three macro-genres (narrative, informational, opinion), modeled after Spectrum Writing. Each atom: `{ grade, genre, lesson_title, model_text, sentence_starters: string[], guided_questions: string[], checklist: string[] }`.

- **Build a passage authoring pipeline** with a standard prompt template (grade, genre, topic, lexile target, question types required, word count) so AI-generation is repeatable, consistent, and reviewable. Include a post-generation validation step that checks: word count within ±15%, question type coverage (≥3 types present), vocabulary density (≤1 stretch word per 50 words for K–2, ≤2 for Grades 3–4).

- **Implement three-level differentiation for Grade 3+ passages**, matching the Cold Reads Developing / On-Level / Advanced model: same weekly skill, three passage lengths (~150 / ~220 / ~300 words), question DOK levels increase across levels (DOK 1–2 / DOK 1–3 / DOK 2–3). This requires generating 3× as many Grade 3–4 passages but makes the app usable across a wider reading range without requiring manual leveling.

- **Map all comprehension questions to CCSS ELA standards** (RL/RI 1.1–4.9) at generation time, storing the standard code in each question object. This enables skills-navigator filtering, quiz-builder integration (matching MathQuest's quiz system architecture), and teacher reporting by standard.

- **For the MAP test-prep strand**, generate short mixed-skill worksheet pages at each RIT band (161–230), with 6–7 micro-tasks per page (passage → question, sequencing, table of contents, genre matching, end-mark, vocabulary morphology, story elements). These can share content atoms with the main comprehension strand rather than requiring separate passage generation.

- **K content requires special handling:** Grade K passages (20–40 words) are below standard Lexile measurement. Design them as visual-first atoms with an audio-read-aloud component, picture-select questions, and touch-to-answer rather than typed input. The click-to-select and picture-match interaction patterns from MathQuest's existing multiple-choice system can be adapted directly.

- **All AI-generated passages must be human-reviewed** before committing to the content bundle. Suggested review checklist: (1) no paraphrasing of identifiable source text, (2) age-appropriate vocabulary for the target Lexile, (3) questions test distinct cognitive skills (no two questions testing the same detail), (4) answer key is unambiguous, (5) topic is culturally neutral and appropriate for K–6 classroom use.

- **Spelling and writing atoms are lower-priority than comprehension passages** for the initial launch, since comprehension has the broadest cross-grade demand and the most direct analog to MathQuest's question-based gameplay. Ship comprehension atoms first; add spelling and writing atoms in subsequent content updates.
