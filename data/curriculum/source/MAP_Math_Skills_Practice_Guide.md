## Purpose
This guide summarizes the math skills and MAP-style task types observed in the notes/photos. It is designed so future students can practise the **same kinds of mathematical thinking and representations** without copying the exact test questions.

## MAP Math Skills / Task Types Observed

| # | Skill / Concept | What students should practise doing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **One-step equations** | Solve a missing-number equation such as `x + 9 = 13`. Understand that the letter represents an unknown value. |
| 2 | **Ordering whole numbers and decimals** | Put several numbers in **least-to-greatest** or **greatest-to-least** order, including mixtures of whole numbers and decimals. |
| 3 | **Ratios from pictures** | Look at groups of shapes and determine a ratio, such as circles to squares or shaded to unshaded. |
| 4A | **Symmetry: comparing different shapes** | Compare **different shapes** and determine whether they have the **same or different numbers of lines of symmetry**. |
| 4B | **Symmetry: same shape, different lines** | Look at the **same shape with different possible symmetry lines drawn on it** and decide which line correctly shows a line of symmetry, or which drawing is incorrect. |
| 5 | **Equal sharing / division from a visual** | Given a collection of objects and a number of people or groups, determine **how many each person or group receives**. |
| 6 | **Multiplication represented by arrays/groups** | Look at an array or equal groups and identify the multiplication equation that represents it. |
| 7 | **Comparing numbers** | Select a number that is **less than** or **greater than** a given number. |
| 8 | **Comparing and ordering decimals** | Compare decimal numbers and arrange several decimals in order. |
| 9 | **Area using unit squares** | Identify a picture that represents a specified area, such as **4 square units**, or count unit squares to determine area. |
| 10 | **Evaluating numerical expressions / order of operations** | Evaluate expressions involving parentheses, multiplication, addition, and subtraction, such as `47 - (2 x 8)`. |
| 11 | **Nets of 3-D shapes** | Look at several 2-D nets and determine **which net will form a particular 3-D solid**. |
| 12 | **Finding an angle from a diagram** | Determine the measure of a missing angle using information shown in a geometric diagram. This may involve the **sum of angles in a triangle**. |
| 13 | **Volume using unit cubes** | Count cubes or use dimensions to determine how many **cubic units** make a solid. |
| 14 | **Multi-digit addition** | Add multi-digit whole numbers, using place value correctly. |
| 15 | **Perimeter** | Given side lengths of a polygon or triangle, calculate the **total distance around the shape**. |
| 16 | **Place-value / base-ten models** | Interpret a visual made from rods/bars and individual squares and determine the number represented. |
| 17 | **Ordering numbers** | Choose the option that correctly shows numbers arranged from **least to greatest** or **greatest to least**. |
| 18 | **Greater than / less than symbols** | Correctly use and interpret `>`, `<`, and `=`. |
| 19 | **Comparing numerical expressions** | Compare multiplication/addition expressions and decide which has the **least or greatest value**, sometimes without fully calculating every expression. |
| 20 | **Multiplying fractions with visual models** | Solve a fraction multiplication problem by using or interpreting a shaded visual model. |
| 21A | **Reading clocks / telling time** | Read analogue and/or digital clocks and identify the represented time. |
| 21B | **Converting minutes to hours** | Convert between minutes and hours, including equivalent forms such as **90 minutes = 1.5 hours = 1 1/2 hours**. |
| 22A | **Line plots / dot plots** | Read a graph or plot and identify a statement, value, or data set that matches the plotted data. |
| 22B | **Adding decimals** | Add decimal numbers correctly by aligning decimal places. |
| 23 | **Addition word problems: receiving/gaining a second quantity** | Read a story in which a person **starts with one amount and then receives/is given another amount**, then determine the new total. Recognize that this is an **addition situation**. |
| 24 | **Division by making equal groups** | Use objects or manipulatives shown in a picture and **move/sort them into equal groups**. Understand division as grouping. |
| 25 | **Ordering and comparing times** | Put digital clock times in chronological order and/or identify times that fall **between two given times**. |
| 26 | **Multiplication through equal groups** | Determine the total when there are several groups containing the same number, such as four groups of 10 pencils. |
| Graph | **Interpreting a point on a coordinate graph** | Explain what an ordered pair means **in context**. Connect the x-coordinate and y-coordinate to their labels and units. |

## Suggested MAP Practice Strands

Rather than treating these as isolated questions, organize practice into seven strands.

### 1. Number & Place Value
- Compare whole numbers and decimals.
- Order numbers from least to greatest and greatest to least.
- Use `>`, `<`, and `=` correctly.
- Interpret base-ten and place-value models.

### 2. Operations & Algebra
- Multi-digit addition.
- Decimal addition.
- One-step equations with unknowns.
- Evaluate numerical expressions.
- Use order of operations.
- Compare the values of different expressions.

### 3. Multiplication & Division
- Interpret arrays and equal groups.
- Match pictures to multiplication equations.
- Share objects equally.
- Make equal groups for division.
- Solve multiplication through repeated equal groups.
- Recognize addition situations in word problems when a second quantity is received or added.

### 4. Fractions & Decimals
- Compare and order decimals.
- Add decimals.
- Multiply fractions.
- Interpret fraction multiplication using visual models.

### 5. Measurement
- Tell time on analogue and digital clocks.
- Convert minutes to hours.
- Order and compare times.
- Find area in square units.
- Find perimeter.
- Find volume in cubic units.

### 6. Geometry
- Compare the number of lines of symmetry in different shapes.
- Decide whether a proposed line is a correct line of symmetry for the same shape.
- Find missing angles from diagrams.
- Use angle relationships in triangles when appropriate.
- Recognize 3-D figures from their nets.

### 7. Data & Graphing
- Interpret line plots and dot plots.
- Read coordinate graphs.
- Explain the meaning of a plotted point in a real-world context.

## Important Pattern in the MAP Tasks

Many of the observed questions are **not simply computation questions**. Students are repeatedly asked to move between representations:

- **picture -> equation**
- **equation -> meaning**
- **graph -> sentence**
- **model -> number**
- **word problem -> operation**
- **shape -> mathematical property**

This means practice should not consist only of calculation worksheets. Students should regularly be asked to interpret models, pictures, diagrams, graphs, and story situations.

## Examples of MAP-Style Practice Prompts

- Which picture shows `4 x 3`?
- Which equation matches this array?
- What does this point on the graph tell you?
- Which model represents this fraction multiplication problem?
- Which net makes this solid?
- Which number is between these two values?
- Which two shapes have the same number of lines of symmetry?
- Which line correctly divides this shape into two matching halves?
- Sara has 9 stickers. Her teacher gives her 4 more. How many stickers does Sara have now?

## Special Focus: Symmetry

Students should practise **both** of these forms of symmetry questions:

1. **Compare different shapes:** Determine whether two or more different shapes have the same or different numbers of lines of symmetry.
2. **Judge lines on the same shape:** Look at the same shape with different proposed symmetry lines and identify which line is correct or incorrect.

## Special Focus: Addition Word Problems

The observed word-problem type is the structure:

**start amount + amount received = total**

Students should learn to recognize that words such as **gets, receives, is given, adds, joins, more** often signal that a second quantity is being added to an initial quantity.

Example:

> Sara has 9 stickers. Her teacher gives her 4 more. How many stickers does Sara have now?

The key skill is not only carrying out the addition. Students must first recognize the **mathematical situation** represented by the story.

## Recommended Emphasis for Future Practice

Based on the repeated task types in the notes, give especially frequent practice with:

- number comparison and ordering;
- decimal comparison and operations;
- visual multiplication and division;
- time and time conversion;
- symmetry and other geometry representations;
- measurement tasks involving area, perimeter, and volume;
- interpreting graphs and plotted points;
- moving between visual models, equations, and written situations.
